Bitcoin basics guide

One idea at a time. Tap Next to go through all six.

Start here1 of 6

1. What is Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is money that lives on the internet.

No bank or company runs it.

People send it straight to each other — anywhere in the world.

Think of it like cash, but for the internet. You hold it yourself, and you can send it at any time of day.

2. You don't need a whole one

You can start with just a few dollars.

One bitcoin divides into 100,000,000 tiny pieces.

Those pieces are called sats — short for “satoshis.”

Try it: the converter at the top of this page shows any dollar amount in sats.

3. Your wallet and your keys

A wallet is an app that holds your keys.

Your keys are what prove the bitcoin is yours — like the only key to a safe.

When you set up a wallet, it gives you recovery words (also called a seed phrase). That is your master backup.

Stamp or engrave them in metal — steel or titanium, made for long-term storage; they survive fire and water. Paper is only ever a temporary step, never the final backup. Keep them secret. Never type them into a website.

Two easy apps to start with are Cash App and Strike.

Get Cash App free: Android · iPhone (by Block, Inc.)

Get Strike free: Android · iPhone (by Zap Solutions)

4. Sending and receiving

To receive, you share your address — or show a code to scan, like the ones on this page.

To send, you paste an address or scan a code, check it, and confirm.

  • Lightning — arrives in seconds, for a tiny fee. Good for small, everyday payments.
  • On-chain — the slower, original way. It settles on the blockchain — that just means the shared record book that everyone can check.

5. Why only 21 million?

There will never be more than 21 million bitcoin. Ever.

New bitcoin are released slowly by mining — computers doing work to add new pages to that record book.

About every four years, the amount of new bitcoin is cut in half. That event is called the halving.

Less new supply, on a set schedule that nobody can change.

6. Stay safe

  • Bitcoin payments can't be undone. Once sent, there is no undo button.
  • Double-check the address. Scan a code instead of typing, when you can.
  • Watch for scams. Anyone promising guaranteed returns, or asking for your recovery words, is trying to steal from you. That is always a scam.
  • Start small while you learn. Send a little first, until it feels easy.

That's the basics. Take your time — you can come back to this guide any time.
